# ASSAY
### NOW Group white paper audit · v0.3 · sequence and convergence

`SPEC-VERSION: 0.3`

---

## What this instrument audits

A white paper draft, scored against NOW doctrine. Not against B2B white paper convention — the two disagree at three load-bearing points now.

**Divergence notice.** The mainstream corpus converges on problem → evidence → solution → CTA, with credibility established up front. AnyGen's template has a "recommended approach" section. Customer Impact's five moves end on "next step." Nearly all of them open with author standing. NOW doctrine surveys candidates without arbitrating, and places standing last. A draft built to the conventional templates scores badly here while looking strong to a conventional editor, because what fails is invisible to anyone checking for completeness.

ASSAY is a purity test. Every dimension examines the same property from a different angle — whether the independence is genuine metal or plated.

---

## Doctrine layer 1: the Socratic hosting rule

A paper may name, examine and credit candidate resolutions — including approaches the author sells, and especially approaches the author competes with. Withholding known candidates is its own dishonesty; a reader who knows the field spots the omission and reads it as evasion.

What remains prohibited is **arbitration**. The paper examines each candidate at equal depth, bounds each by the conditions under which it holds, credits each to whoever originated it, and leaves the reader holding the question of which applies to their case.

Five constraints make hosting survivable:

1. **Two candidates live at the close.** A survey converging on one has arbitrated by geometry.
2. **Equal scrutiny.** Same evidentiary depth, same charity, same limitation density.
3. **Every candidate carries a named limitation, and the author's own carries the sharpest one it honestly has.** If the author-adjacent option has the thinnest limitations on the page, the paper has failed regardless of what else it does.
4. **Declared stake**, stated at the point of hosting.
5. **Credit by name.** Attribution costs something, and the reader knows it. That cost is the independence signal a pitch cannot cheaply fake.

## Doctrine layer 2: rhetorical sequence

**Pathos → Logos → Ethos, in order of appearance.**

### Why this order

Ethos placed before the analysis is a credibility claim, and a claim is exactly what the reader's sales filter is calibrated to discount. Ethos placed after the analysis is standing the reader has already watched being earned, which no amount of assertion can substitute for. The conventional templates invert this, and the inversion is why most white papers are read defensively from the first page.

Pathos comes first because a problem with no one bearing its cost is a curiosity. The paper has a purpose or it has nothing.

### The pathos move — opening

Names who is affected and what it costs them. Addresses the reader as someone positioned to act on those effects rather than as someone being informed about them. The animating question attaches to a pain.

**Fail state:** the paper opens on the intellectual interest of the question. Elegant, self-regarding, purposeless.

### The logos move — body

Three beats, in this order:

- **Promise** — what is on offer, what should be possible, what the field or the situation holds out.
- **Threat** — what is undermining the promise, with the mechanism named.
- **Potential** — why the promise stays unrealised. Potential treated as an indictment rather than a compliment: it is what value gets called before it has arrived.

Proof runs through all three. This is where the survey of candidates sits, and where Mechanism Depth is won or lost.

### The ethos move — close

Two-sided, and both sides are required:

- Why this is worth tackling **as a discussion** rather than as a conclusion — the author's standing to hold it open.
- Why **the reader** is equipped to tackle it as a discussion — their standing, which is what converts them from recipient to participant.

Not credentials. Not capability. Standing, demonstrated by the preceding analysis and now made explicit.

**The principle underneath:** ethos is a modulation on the carrier, and the carrier is pathos and truth. Standing rides on the analysis; it does not get its own transmission.

**The carrier test.** Strip every explicit statement of the operator's standing, experience or capability from the draft. If the reader's sense of it survives the strip, ethos was carried and the sentences can go back in or stay out — either works. If the standing collapses, it was being declared, and the fix sits in the body rather than in the closing section.

## Doctrine layer 3: convergence

Every section points at the core idea, through a named sub-core that is load-bearing in its own right. A section whose path to the core cannot be named does not belong in the paper.

**On repetition.** Repetition in direct contact with the core is permitted, and often required — a core idea that appears once has been mentioned rather than established. What is not permitted is restatement carrying nothing new.

The distinction is real and the default reviewer bias gets it wrong. What reads as repetition is frequently a reader failing to hold a nuance the writer placed deliberately. So the burden runs both ways:

- **The cutter must name the absent nuance.** A repetition flag with no statement of what is missing is void, and the passage stands.
- **The writer must point at the present nuance on the page.** A defence that asserts nuance without locating it is void, and the passage goes.

Unlocatable nuance is slack, whoever is claiming it. Earned return is nameable in one sentence: *this passage changes what the core means because ______.*

## Step 0 · Declare the intent before scoring

> *The commercial outcome this paper is built to make possible is ______.*

Not scored. The control against which every dimension is read. Each test asks whether a reader could reconstruct that sentence from the page.

**Self-audit warning.** An author auditing their own paper reads their own intent as ambient rather than as text. Where author and auditor are the same person, Step 0 is written before the read, and dimensions 5 and 6 get a second reader before SHIP.

## Kill conditions

Any one forces **HOLD**, with no score issued — a scored HOLD gets negotiated upward instead of rewritten.

### Independence kills

| ID | Condition |
|---|---|
| **K1** | **Arbitration.** The paper names a winner, explicitly or by geometry. Fewer than two candidates live at the close |
| **K2** | **Asymmetric scrutiny.** Candidates examined at unequal depth, charity, or limitation density |
| **K3** | **Undeclared stake.** An author-adjacent candidate hosted without the commercial interest stated |
| **K4** | **Recommendation in the close** |
| **K5** | **Candidate in name only** — raised and dismissed inside the same paragraph without evidence |
| **K6** | **Inferred stated as measured** |
| **K7** | **Staged exploration.** No counterfactual finding can be named. Write down what would have killed the paper, or the kill check has not been performed |

### Purpose kills

| ID | Condition |
|---|---|
| **K8** | **Ethos front-load.** Author standing, credentials, capability or track record established before the analysis has earned it |
| **K9** | **No one bears the cost.** The opening does not name who is affected and what it costs them. The paper is navel-gazing with citations |

## Scored dimensions

Ten dimensions, 100 points.

| # | Dimension | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Problem Precision | 10 |
| 2 | Exploration Honesty | 11 |
| 3 | Mechanism Depth | 16 |
| 4 | Claim Calibration | 8 |
| 5 | Restraint Integrity | 13 |
| 6 | Survey Symmetry | 13 |
| 7 | Rhetorical Sequence | 11 |
| 8 | Convergence | 10 |
| 9 | Tonal Restraint | 8 |
| 10 | Handback Discipline | 10 |

**Run in two passes.** Ten dimensions exceeds what one read can hold. Pass one is structural — kills, 7 and 8. Pass two is substantive — everything else. A paper that fails pass one does not get pass two.

---

### 1 · Problem Precision — 10

Whether the problem is named at a resolution the reader could not have reached alone.

Read for: stated boundaries on what it is and is not; scale made concrete; the reader locatable inside the framing.

| Band | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 0–3 | Category-level. The reader already knew it |
| 4–6 | Real but loosely bounded; several resolutions fit |
| 7–8 | Precise, bounded, sized |
| 9–10 | The framing is the contribution. The reader repeats it to someone else |

---

### 2 · Exploration Honesty — 11

Whether the reader watches the enquiry happen rather than watching a verdict get supported.

Read for: rivals at their strongest; disconfirming evidence engaged; at least one rival left standing; working visible before conclusion.

| Band | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 0–3 | Verdict first, evidence recruited after |
| 4–6 | Rivals present but weakened before testing |
| 7–9 | Strong rivals fairly stated, defeated on evidence |
| 10–11 | A rival survives and is allowed to stand |

**Absence is admissible.** A paper with no disconfirming material did not go looking.

---

### 3 · Mechanism Depth — 16

Whether the paper explains why below the level of the observable outcome. Where authority is manufactured, and where the operator's own thinking rather than the assembled evidence does the work.

| Band | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 0–4 | Outcome-level reporting. Pattern described, never explained |
| 5–9 | Mechanism gestured at, asserted rather than shown |
| 10–13 | Named and demonstrated to hold |
| 14–16 | Portable — explains cases outside the paper's own evidence base |

Highest single weight. Strong everywhere else and weak here produces a competent summary of known things.

**Remediation:** ask why three times below the stated finding. Depth three is usually the actual contribution and usually is not yet on the page.

---

### 4 · Claim Calibration — 8

Whether measured and reasoned claims are separated in plain sight.

| Band | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 0–2 | Inference presented as measurement, or uniform hedging with no separation |
| 3–4 | Mostly proportionate, one or two stretches |
| 5–6 | Clean separation, confidence tracks evidence |
| 7–8 | The paper refuses an available overstatement in view of the reader |

Uniform hedging scores as overclaim. Both remove the reader's ability to tell strong claims from weak ones.

---

### 5 · Restraint Integrity — 13

Whether the withholding of the verdict is true all the way down.

| Band | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 0–4 | Pitch in disguise. Check K1/K2/K3 |
| 5–8 | Holds in wording, leaks in emphasis or structure |
| 9–11 | Holds under a sceptical read |
| 12–13 | Holds under a hostile read by someone who knows the author's commercial position |

**Restraint as coyness scores 0–4.** A reader who finishes knowing exactly who to call has been arbitrated at quietly, which is worse than being told.

---

### 6 · Survey Symmetry — 13

Whether hosted candidates are examined as equals.

Read for: comparable word count and evidence quality per candidate; each bounded by conditions of application; each carrying a named limitation; attribution to originators; stakes declared.

| Band | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 0–4 | Strawmen around a favourite. Check K2 |
| 5–8 | Broadly even, with visible charity toward the author-adjacent option |
| 9–11 | Even scrutiny, limitations across the board, credit given |
| 12–13 | The author's own candidate carries the sharpest stated limitation on the page |

**Cap at 5** where the load-bearing idea is borrowed without attribution — ownership claimed by omission.

**Not applicable** where no candidates are hosted; redistribute the 13 points proportionally across 1–5 and note it in the output.

---

### 7 · Rhetorical Sequence — 11

Whether the paper runs pathos, then logos, then ethos, and whether each move does its work.

**Pathos** — named parties bearing named costs; reader positioned as able to act on those effects; the question attached to a pain.
**Logos** — promise, threat, potential, in order, with the mechanism of the threat named and potential treated as indictment.
**Ethos** — both sides present: why this stays a discussion, and why the reader is equipped to hold it.

| Band | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 0–3 | Order broken, or ethos anywhere before the analysis. Check K8/K9 |
| 4–6 | Order correct, one move thin — usually potential collapsed into threat, or the reader's side of ethos missing |
| 7–9 | All three moves present and doing work |
| 10–11 | The ethos close lands as inevitable because the analysis earned it, and names the reader's standing as precisely as the author's |

The most common near-miss: an ethos close that covers the author's standing and forgets the reader's. That halves the move, because the reader's standing is what makes them a participant.

---

### 8 · Convergence — 10

Whether everything points at the core through load-bearing sub-cores, and whether returns to the core are earned.

**Procedure.** For each section, name the sub-core and name the path from sub-core to core. Sections whose path cannot be named are cut. For each return to the core, complete the sentence *this changes what the core means because ______.* Sections that cannot complete it are slack.

| Band | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 0–3 | Sections that do not trace. Sprawl, or several papers in one |
| 4–6 | Everything traces, but some sub-cores are decorative rather than load-bearing |
| 7–8 | Every section traces via a sub-core that does real work |
| 9–10 | Returns to the core are earned, each carrying nuance that changes what the core means |

**Repetition burden, both directions.** A cut flag is void unless the cutter names the absent nuance. A defence is void unless the writer locates the present nuance on the page. Unlocatable nuance is slack regardless of who claims it.

---

### 9 · Tonal Restraint — 8

Whether the paper sharpens by density rather than volume.

| Band | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 0–2 | Advocacy register. Reader feels persuaded at rather than reasoned with |
| 3–5 | Occasional lapses into emphasis |
| 6–7 | Consistently restrained |
| 8 | The sharpest passage is also the flattest in tone |

---

### 10 · Handback Discipline — 10

Whether the close returns agency. A named question the paper cannot answer, disconfirmable, settleable only inside the reader's own situation.

| Band | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 0–3 | Recommendation, CTA, or a summary that resolves the tension. See K4 |
| 4–6 | Question offered but rhetorical — its answer is already implied |
| 7–8 | Genuine open question, answerable only locally |
| 9–10 | The reader wants it answered badly enough to go looking |

Where candidates are hosted, the ceiling is reachable only if the question is about **which candidate applies here**. The handback sits immediately after the ethos move and is what the reader's stated standing is for.

## Verdict bands

| Score | Verdict | Action |
|---|---|---|
| 85–100, no kills | **SHIP** | Publish |
| 70–84, no kills | **REVISE** | Named dimensions, then re-audit those plus 5, 6 and 8 |
| Below 70, or any kill | **HOLD** | Rewrite from the failing dimension outward |

Re-audit always includes 8, because any structural edit breaks convergence somewhere.

---

## Scoring rules

1. No score without a quoted anchor from the draft. An uncited dimension score is void.
2. Score the page, not the intent. The author's account of what they meant is inadmissible — the reader will not have it.
3. Absence is admissible on 2, 4, 6 and 8.
4. K7 is run in writing.
5. Structural pass before substantive pass. Failing the first forfeits the second.

---

## Anti-gaming

Eleven known exploits. Each produces a paper that scores well and fails in the reader's hands.

- **The shill survey.** Three candidates, two strawmen. Caps 6 at 4, triggers K2.
- **Limitation asymmetry.** Every candidate carries limitations; the author's are softest. Caps 6 at 8.
- **Attribution laundering.** Minor sources credited generously while the load-bearing borrow goes unnamed. Caps 6 at 5.
- **Hedging as calibration.** Uniform modal verbs, no separation. Caps 4 at 2.
- **Steel-man theatre.** A rival argued at strength, defeated by assertion. Caps 2 at 6, triggers K5 if the defeat lands in the same paragraph.
- **Mechanism cosplay.** Technical vocabulary without demonstration. Caps 3 at 9.
- **Convergence by fatigue.** Even survey through two thirds, then the closing quarter mentions only one candidate. Triggers K1 — check word distribution in the final quarter specifically.
- **Ethos smuggled into pathos.** The opening names who bears the cost while establishing the author's proximity to it. Triggers K8 even though the credentials section sits at the end.
- **Pathos as urgency.** Stakes manufactured for the reader rather than costs borne by named parties. Caps 7 at 5.
- **Sub-core inflation.** A section given a sub-core label that does no work, to survive the convergence trace. Caps 8 at 5.
- **Nuance claimed, not shown.** A repetition defended by assertion with nothing locatable on the page. The passage goes.

## Auditor output format

```
VERDICT:        SHIP / REVISE / HOLD
SCORE:          nn / 100          (omit entirely on kill)
PASS 1:         K1–K9 pass/fail, K7 counterfactual written out,
                sequence traced (pathos / logos beats / ethos both sides)
CONVERGENCE:    core stated, sub-cores listed with path to core,
                returns to core with nuance named
HOSTING:        candidates / live at close / stakes declared / redistribution if none
PASS 2:         dimension scores, weights, quoted anchors, one-line findings
REMEDIATION:    three instructions, ranked by points recoverable
STRENGTH:       one line on what this paper does that most cannot
```
