# Nurture Framework

Framing the follow-up email sequences that run after the quiz, routed by result.

## Table of contents

3. Sequence shapes

## 1. One sequence per result type

Each band gets its own sequence, matched to where that person is:

- **Not-ready band → education-first, slow cadence.** Build readiness and trust; no hard sell. Lead them toward the takeaway tool and the steps that move them into the target band, with a "re-take the scorecard" loop for re-engagement when their situation changes.
- **Target band → conversion-focused cadence.** Acknowledge their result, deep-dive their top gap, layer social proof and case studies, pre-empt the known objection, and drive the primary CTA (e.g. booking) with escalating but respectful urgency.
- **Over-qualified band → light-touch, insight-led.** High-value content, minimal ask, a soft "second set of eyes" offer, then a slow stay-warm cadence. Low priority by design if these rarely convert/refer.

## 2. How routing reaches the email platform

ScoreApp passes the result/tier (and optionally specific answers and category scores) to the connected email/CRM platform via the integration's field maps and start/finish tags. Each sequence is triggered by that value. Pass the intent answers as extra fields so a human can prioritise outreach within the conversion sequence by readiness. Confirm the specific integration's capabilities in Step 0 — field mapping and tagging differ per platform.

## 3. Sequence shapes

A workable default for the conversion sequence (adapt timings to the sales cycle):
- Instant: result recap + primary CTA + takeaway tool link
- Day 1: deep-dive on their #1 gap + how to act on it
- Day 3: client success story + CTA reminder + objection pre-frame
- Week 1: a flagship case study (ideally video)
- Week 2: cost-of-delay / why-now angle
- Week 3–4: decision frameworks and lifestyle/outcome framing
- Week 6: final direct invite + testimonials + objection pre-frame
- Week 8: soft transition to general content + periodic re-engagement

Education-first and light-touch sequences are shorter and slower; keep the not-ready sequence genuinely useful (it's the band whose result page should be the *most* informative).

## 4. The takeaway-asset touchpoint

If the funnel includes a takeaway tool or lead magnet, deliver it in the first email of every relevant sequence as well as on the result page. Two touchpoints for one asset increases use and gives the email a concrete, non-salesy reason to exist.
