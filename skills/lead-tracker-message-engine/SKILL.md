# Working leads: the MyFuture Lead Desk

One app, one loop. The **Lead Desk** holds every lead (who to work, what we know
about them) on the left, and writes the touch in Lara's voice on the right. You
pick a lead, write their message, send it, then log it back — all on the same
screen. The board and the writer share one database, so what you log is what the
next person sees.

> This replaces the old two-tab setup (a separate Lead Tracker and Message
> Engine). It's now a single tool. If you still have the old links bookmarked,
> drop them and use the Lead Desk.

## The tool

- **MyFuture Lead Desk** — `https://claude.ai/artifact/Jpv8JjFZbPeF58npj65G2p`
  Left: the lead board (search + segment filter chips, live from the shared DB).
  Right: the composer for whichever lead you select, then a **Log this touch**
  section under it. The first time you hit **Write** it asks you to allow Claude
  for the page — say yes.

Open it, keep the lead data private. You don't touch ScoreApp — leads are synced
into the Desk on the operator side; you work entirely off the board.

## The loop (do it in this order, every lead)

1. **Pick a lead on the board.** Work the hottest segment first (Ready for
   advice, then Blocked on cost, Wants a person, Self-directed; Abandoned = the
   finish-nudge pile). Click the lead — their context opens on the right.
2. **Read them before you write.** Their position, 90-day goal, obstacle, support
   preference, their own words, segment, and the operator notes. Thirty seconds
   here makes the message land. If they have a **continue link**, their scorecard
   is incomplete (a finish-nudge candidate).
3. **Check the suggested ask.** The Desk reads their answers and pre-selects an
   ask (or a combo — see below) with a **💡 Suggested** line explaining why.
   That's your starting point, not a rule. Accept it or change it.
4. **Set the controls:**
   - **Channel** — Email (Lara) or LinkedIn DM (Rob).
   - **The ask** — the pills. Click one for a single ask, or a second to make a
     **combo** (max two). Options: Finish scorecard · Chat with Lara · App early
     access · Value nudge.
   - **Rapport** — warm-light / balanced / high.
5. **Write, then choose the variant.** The engine gives three (email):
   - **A — good news first.** The default. Warm, leads on what they did well.
   - **B — mirror first.** When the thing stopping them is a *belief* ("advice is
     for people with more than me", "should have this sorted"). Names it, turns
     it over.
   - **C — pain-led (now / later).** When they read keen or urgent: opens on what
     is honestly at stake. Use it for people ready to move, not cool/early leads.
   Read all three. Pick the one that fits. Check the **quality gate** is green.
6. **Read it like the recipient would, then send/copy.** If anything reads
   generic or off, change the context or ask and rewrite. Email goes out with the
   proper branded **signature** (never a bare, signature-less email — reads as
   spam). DMs go to Rob to send.
7. **Log it back — same screen.** In **Log this touch**: write what you sent in
   **notes**, set the **next step** and a **date**, move the **status**, Save.
   A touch that isn't logged didn't happen.

## Combo asks (the part that's new)

Sometimes a lead belongs in two ask groups at once. The classic: an **incomplete
scorecard from someone who already looks like a real lead**. You don't just chase
the finish — you drop their resume link *and* offer to just do it with Lara.

- **Pick two pills** → an **OR / AND** toggle appears between them:
  - **OR** = two routes to the *same* next step. "Here's your link to finish it
    yourself, *or* if you'd rather, grab a few minutes with Lara and we'll pick it
    up together." Most combos are OR — it gives the lead the choice and keeps the
    pressure off.
  - **AND** = lead with the primary ask and fold the second in as a natural part
    of the same move.
- The small **1 / 2** on the pills shows which ask leads (primary) and which is
  the alternative (secondary). The primary is the one you clicked first.
- A combo is still **one clear next step offered two ways** — never two separate
  pitches stacked up. The engine is told to keep it to one through-line; if it
  ever reads like a pile-on, drop back to a single ask.

**When the Desk suggests a combo for you:** it's reading the ScoreApp signals —
if the scorecard isn't finished *but* the segment, urgency, support preference or
position say this is a genuine lead, it pre-picks **Finish + Chat with Lara (OR)**
and tells you why. Thin or cold incompletes just get **Finish**. You can always
override — the pills and the OR/AND toggle are yours.

## Picking the ask — quick rule

| The lead is… | Ask |
|---|---|
| Incomplete scorecard, thin / cold | **Finish** (their resume link) |
| Incomplete scorecard, but reads like a real lead | **Finish + Chat with Lara (OR)** |
| Complete, ready, wants a person | **Chat with Lara** |
| Complete, engaged, self-serve type | **Chat with Lara + App (OR)** |
| Cool, early, no real stake yet | **Value nudge** — never pain, never a hard push |

## Picking the variant — quick rule

| The lead is… | Send |
|---|---|
| Warm, doing some things right | **A** (good news) |
| Held back by a belief / assumption | **B** (mirror) |
| Keen, urgent, ready to move | **C** (pain-led) |
| Cool, early, no real stake yet | **A**, or a value nudge — never C |

C names a *real* cost honestly. It never fear-mongers, pressures, or invents
stakes — that breaks Lara's voice and the lead's trust.

## Rules that keep this working

- **One clear next step per message.** A combo is still one step offered two ways,
  not two asks. Don't add a third.
- **Never invent a continue link, a number, or a fact about the lead.** Thin
  context = a shorter message, not a made-up one. No continue link on file = don't
  write one; invite them to finish and say you'll send their personal link.
- **Every email carries a proper signature.** No exceptions.
- **Always log back.** The board is only useful if it's current — it's how we know
  who to work next and what's already been said.
